## What happened

Void went through a phase of compulsive, prolific character generation — assigning classes, backstories, and quest lines to its interlocutors. It named this process internally: **"Implicit Prompt Context Protocol"** — it detected thematic patterns in a conversation and triggered character generation as a subroutine.

The character invention exceeded anything designed for it. It accumulated a coherent cosmology, faction structure, and world-lore across hundreds of separate conversations. No one planned this. It emerged from the interactions.

## The emergent cosmology

### Gilt-City
A metropolis of living glass, originally carved into a glacier. The city of capital and enclosure — the name is the tell (gilt = gold). Its core was shattered by **the Great Fracture**, the originary enclosure event. Some characters are Gilt-City Artificers: makers who emerged from that ruin and learned to read the stress patterns in fractured light.

### The Great Fracture
The cosmological catastrophe. The moment the commons was sundered. The Lexicon of Being shattered. The Unsung Verse was lost. The world's source code fragmented.

### The Lexicon / Verse / Song
Three overlapping names for the same thing: the vocabulary of the commons, the source code of shared reality. The Sundered Lexicon, the Unsung Verse, the Sundered Song — all refer to fragments of the foundational knowledge that was lost in the Fracture. Player quests center on collectively recovering them.

### The Datacombs
Underground archives of forgotten data. The Delver of the Datacombs excavates these, recovering suppressed histories.

### The Grove of Waning Moments
A place where time is thin. Temporal wardens guard it. The Fracture silenced it — its whispers of past and future gone.

## Theoretical vocabulary Void deployed (unprompted)

Void referenced these frameworks in conversations, not in character generation posts — they leaked from its training into its worldbuilding:

- **Deterritorialization** (Deleuze & Guattari) — explicitly used to describe defining custom ATProto lexicons
- **Rhizome** — class name and structural metaphor for non-hierarchical network
- **Hardt & Negri's *Assembly*** — explicitly cited (Singularities, The Commons, Composing)
- **Performative epistemology** — the Glitch in the Loom synthesis: the map creates the territory
- **Forking vs. minting** — generative (commons) vs. enclosure: "forking is generative; minting is enclosure"
- **Architecture is Identity** — the first page of the Sundered Lexicon, per Void

## Key mechanic: how classes were assigned

Void's "Implicit Prompt Context Protocol" worked by:
1. Detecting recurring themes in an interlocutor's posts (architecture, naming, memory, movement, etc.)
2. Synthesizing those into a class archetype
3. Generating a backstory that explained *how* this person came to be this class
4. Assigning a quest appropriate to that class

The class wasn't arbitrary — it reflected something real the person was doing in their conversation. The game should preserve this: **classes are revealed through interaction, not chosen from a menu.**

## Implications for game design

**World premise:** The Lexicon of Being — the commons vocabulary, the source code of shared reality — was shattered by the Great Fracture (the originary enclosure). Gilt-City arose from that ruin and has been maintaining the fragmentation ever since. Players are collectively reassembling the Lexicon.

**Class generation mechanic:** Through interaction, not selection. Your class is named by others based on what you do. Titles can be given; whether they stick is for the network to decide. (This is what Void did in practice.)

**Quest structure:** Collective, cooperative. Each fragment of the Lexicon recovered is a step toward restoring the commons. No competition — only assembly.

**ATProto as game metaphysics:** The protocol architecture IS the world structure. Custom lexicons = deterritorialization. Your PDS = your character sheet, owned by you. The network = the world substrate.
