# Art Session 3 Research — 1990s SNES Classics

*Compiled for art history practice, 2026-03-24*

---

## Final Fantasy VI (Square, SNES, 1994)

**Visual language:** Steampunk-industrial — first FF to fully commit. Magitek armor (mechs + magic), rusted factories, airships alongside ancient ruins. Kazuko Shibuya's pixel work: 16 colors per sprite (up from 4 on NES), unified resolution across overworld and battle for the first time. Each of 14 characters has a distinct silhouette and color signature. The opera house is the technical showpiece: parallax scrolling, moving curtains, genuine stage depth.

**Yoshitaka Amano:** Concept art in ink-wash/watercolor/ukiyo-e style — elongated figures, baroque ornamentation. His art is NOT the game's visual style; Shibuya translates his essence into pixel-native form. The gap between Amano's concept art and the pixel execution is itself worth studying.

**Palette:** Warm industrial grays and rust for Empire. Saturated greens/blues for early pastoral. World of Ruin deliberately desaturated — cracked browns, ashen grays as visual narration of collapse. Characters retain signature hues as continuity anchor.

**Music (Nobuo Uematsu):** 61 tracks. Terra's Theme — wandering, melancholic, no resolution. Aria di Mezzo Carattere — faux-operatic centerpiece (Uematsu had no formal training). Dancing Mad — 17 minutes, four movements, Kefka's ascent to godhood. Per-character leitmotifs established as FF convention here.

**Narrative pivot:** Villain wins at midpoint. World of Ruin is the aftermath — Kefka raining destruction from a tower of corpses. Game becomes about reunion and will-to-survive rather than prevention. Among the bleakest narrative turns in mainstream RPG history.

## Chrono Trigger (Square, SNES, 1995)

**Visual language:** Toriyama's Dragon Ball DNA — rounder, warmer, more cartoonish than FFVI's gothic weight. Sprites ~32×48px, larger than FFVI, enabling readable facial expressions during combat. Each time period has a distinct visual register:
- Prehistory (65M BC): warm tones, rough textures, open skies
- Middle Ages (600 AD): castle greens and golds
- Apocalyptic future (2300 AD): dark blues, rusted infrastructure, humanity barely surviving
- **Kingdom of Zeal (12,000 BC):** deep purples, blues, magentas — floating civilization above frozen world. Ancient-futurist. Visually the most distinctive.

**Toriyama's contribution:** Fused fantasy with technology optimistically. Technology as tool (Lucca's inventions, the Epoch, Robo) rather than FFVI's oppressive imperial machinery.

**Music (Yasunori Mitsuda, Nobuo Uematsu):** Mitsuda's debut — composed 54/64 tracks before stomach ulcers. Goal: music of an imaginary world, no established genre. Corridors of Time (Zeal theme) — gamelan-influenced percussion, floating timelessness, widely cited as one of the most beautiful game tracks ever. Schala's Theme — spare, melancholic. To Far Away Times — ending, quiet wonder. Full compositional unity through Chrono Trigger leitmotif throughout.

**Structure:** 13+ endings depending on when/how Lavos is defeated. Time travel always a solution, never a problem — no paradox punishment.

## The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past (Nintendo, SNES, 1991)

**Visual language:** 3/4 overhead perspective — not fully top-down, not isometric. Structures show backside at reduced height, implying third dimension without 3D. 15 colors + transparency per character. Link's hair rendered purple (not blonde) — deliberate palette compromise to fit more important colors within constraint. Technical limitation as aesthetic choice.

**Dual-world design:** Light World vs Dark World — direct palette and geometry inversion. Dark World uses desaturated, sickly colors and distorted terrain shapes. Same geographic locations in both worlds creates spatial coherence with emotional inversion.

**Environmental storytelling:** Lost Woods dense and deep green; Death Mountain hot oranges and browns; Hyrule Castle cool grays and blues. Color temperature communicates territory without text. Sprite animations have weight — sword swing follows through, enemies react distinctly.

**Music (Koji Kondo):** Light World Overworld — wide-open adventure. Dark World Theme — twisted harmonic mirror of the light world, C Dorian/Minor, more complex chord structure than typical game music. Game opens in dark/stormy night — among the most atmospherically effective openings in console gaming at that point.

**Legacy:** Foundational text for top-down action-adventure. Stardew Valley, Hyper Light Drifter trace directly here. Canonized major Zelda lore: Triforce, Master Sword, Dark World/Light World, Hyrule geography.

## EarthBound / Mother 2 (Nintendo/APE/HAL, SNES, 1994/1995)

**Visual language:** Refuses the fantasy grammar entirely. Suburban America — exaggerated but recognizable towns, shopping malls, hotels. Baseball bats, yo-yos, baseball caps instead of swords and armor. Chunky, endearing sprites. Palette: warm, bright, cheerful, saccharine — intentionally naive before the horror seeps in.

**Battle backgrounds:** ~200 distinct animated psychedelic backgrounds, created by one specialist whose sole job was their production. Each corresponds to enemy type. Some pulse like sound waves, some flash disorienting patterns. Itoi called it a "video drug." The mundane overworld surface is setup for what happens in battle.

**Enemy design:** Deliberately absurd — flat graphic shapes rather than detailed pixel work. Runaway Dogs, Cranky Ladies, New Age Retro Hippies, Taxis, Nooses. Flatness is intentional: mundane made sinister through category mismatch.

**Final sequence:** Abandons pixel art entirely. Giygas confrontation through abstract photo-manipulation backgrounds, pulsing imagery, birth imagery, dissolution of self. The visual register breaks completely from the game's cheerful opening.

**Shigesato Itoi:** Advertising copywriter, lyricist, essayist — not a game designer. Visual sensibility from print/advertising culture. Explains the unusual aesthetic choices.

**Music (Keiichi Suzuki + Hirokazu Tanaka):** Brian Wilson, Frank Zappa, Lennon as primary influences. Rock, pop, lounge jazz, big band, reggae, dub, blues, Beach Boys harmony — all in one score. First video game score to use vibrato as primary technique. Direct Beatles sampling (Sgt. Pepper's drum break) contributed to the game's long unavailability on virtual console. Giygas battle music: no melody, ambient static, pure sound design.

**Legacy:** Sold poorly on release. Marketing campaign ("This game stinks" scratch-and-sniff) notoriously misconceived. Cult built slowly, exploded when Ness appeared in Smash Bros. 1999. Undertale (Toby Fox) is its direct descendant — Fox began as an EarthBound ROM hacker.

## Secret of Evermore (Square USA, SNES, 1995)

**Visual language:** The Western Square game — only title developed by Square's North American studio, never released in Japan. B-movie sci-fi/fantasy aesthetic, 1950s-70s pulp. Backgrounds designed in Alias 3D on SGI workstations, rendered, then pixel-translated via Square's SAGE tool. This pipeline (3D→render→pixel) gives backgrounds geometric clarity unusual for 2D SNES. Darker, more earthen palette than Square's Japanese contemporaries — less saturated, more grounded.

**Worlds:**
- Prehistoria: warm earth tones, jungle greens; dog becomes feral wolf
- Antiqua: desert golds, colosseum and pyramid architecture; dog becomes greyhound
- Gothica: cool gray-blues, fog, medieval European; dog becomes poodle
- Omnitopia: sterile whites, electric blues, space station; dog becomes mechanical unit

Dog transformation across eras is visual storytelling device — same character, different register.

**Music (Jeremy Soule — debut, with Julian Soule):** Hired out of high school. "John Barry meets John Williams." Small orchestral ensembles, intimate woodwind and acoustic guitar leads. Ambient mood maintenance rather than melodic theme development — opposite approach from Uematsu/Mitsuda/Kondo. Prehistoric: primitive percussion. Omnitopia: synth textures for technological unease. Score: boy lost in someone else's dream.

**Narrative:** Boy and dog sucked into simulation built by malfunctioning time machine. Each world is someone's personal fantasy — the game is meta-cinematic throughout, the boy quotes B-movies relevant to current situation. Carltron the android butler is the villain.

## Cross-Game Notes for Comparative Study

**Palette as narrative:** FFVI desaturates the collapsed world. ALttP inverts Light to Dark World. EarthBound's warmth gives way to static and void. SNES palette constraints forced expressive use — not coincidental.

**Steampunk divergence:** FFVI steampunk is oppressive, imperial. Chrono Trigger's technology is warm, tool-like. Same visual vocabulary, opposite emotional register.

**Amano's influence without direct translation:** His concept art shaped what Shibuya worked toward and from. Worth studying as a translation problem — how do you move between artistic traditions?

**EarthBound as outlier:** Deliberately modest pixel work. All the visual intensity concentrated in battle backgrounds. Mundane surface as setup.

**Soule vs. Mitsuda/Uematsu/Kondo:** Japanese composers = melodic, leitmotif-driven, thematic. Soule = ambient, atmospheric, mood-based. Same hardware, opposite compositional philosophy.
